# Lesson 3: Consumer Equilibrium - Utility Analysis

## Overview

This chapter covers the cardinal utility approach to consumer behavior analysis, often referred to as Marshallian utility analysis. It explains how consumers maximize satisfaction given their income and prices of goods, assuming utility is measurable in cardinal numbers (utils).

**Chapter Reference**: Class 11 Microeconomics (VK Ohri Textbook)
**Duration**: ~45 minutes
**Quiz Questions**: Included in the Quiz section

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, students will be able to:

1.  Define who a consumer is and their main objective.
2.  Understand the concepts of Total Utility (TU) and Marginal Utility (MU).
3.  Explain the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (DMU) with its assumptions.
4.  Determine consumer equilibrium in case of a single commodity.
5.  Determine consumer equilibrium in case of two commodities (Law of Equi-Marginal Utility).
6.  Critique the utility analysis approach by understanding its limitations.

## Key Concepts

### Total Utility (TU)
The sum total of utility derived from the consumption of all units of a commodity.
`TU = ΣMU`

### Marginal Utility (MU)
The additional utility derived from the consumption of one more unit of the given commodity.
`MU = TUn - TUn-1`

### Consumer Equilibrium (One Good)
Condition: `MUx / Px = MUm` (or simply `MUx = Px` if MUm=1)
