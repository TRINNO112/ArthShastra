## Key Concepts

### Indifference Curve
An indifference curve shows all combinations of two goods that give the consumer equal satisfaction. The consumer is indifferent between any points on the same curve.

### Properties of Indifference Curves

| Property | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| Downward Sloping | To maintain same satisfaction, more of one good requires less of another |
| Convex to Origin | Due to diminishing MRS |
| Non-intersecting | If they intersect, it violates transitivity of preferences |
| Higher = Better | Curves further from origin represent higher utility levels |

### Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)
```
MRSxy = Δy/Δx = MUx/MUy
```
MRS measures the rate at which a consumer is willing to substitute good Y for good X while maintaining the same level of satisfaction.

### Budget Line Equation
```
M = Px·X + Py·Y
```
Where:
- M = Consumer's income
- Px, Py = Prices of goods X and Y
- X, Y = Quantities of goods

### Consumer Equilibrium Conditions
1. **Tangency Condition**: MRSxy = Px/Py
2. **Budget Constraint**: The chosen point must lie on the budget line

## Comparison: Cardinal vs Ordinal Approach

| Aspect | Cardinal Utility | Ordinal Utility (IC) |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Utility Measurement | Measurable (utils) | Ranking only |
| Approach | Marshallian | Hicks & Allen |
| Psychological Assumptions | Strong | Weak |
| Applicability | Single & Two Commodities | Two commodities |
| Realism | Less realistic | More realistic |

## Mathematical Formulas

1. **Budget Line Slope**: -Px/Py
2. **MRS**: Δy/Δx = MUx/MUy
3. **Equilibrium Condition**: MRSxy = Px/Py
4. **Budget Constraint**: Px·X + Py·Y = M
